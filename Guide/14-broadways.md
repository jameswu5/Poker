# Chapter 14 — Broadways & Offsuit Holdings

This chapter covers the big-card hands that aren't quite premium: **offsuit broadways** (KQo, KJo, QJo, AJo, ATo, KTo, QTo, JTo) and, more broadly, the **offsuit holdings** that make **top pairs with kicker problems**. These hands are the mirror image of the suited connectors in Chapter 13. Where speculative hands make disguised nuts and *win big, lose small*, broadways make *top pairs that are often dominated* and tend to **win small and lose big** — the signature of **reverse implied odds** (Chapter 2.5). They're playable and even profitable, but only if you respect their two defining weaknesses: **domination** and **poor playability when offsuit**. This chapter is largely about *not overplaying them*.

---

## 14.1 The Two Weaknesses: Domination and Offsuit Playability

**Domination.** A hand is *dominated* when it shares a card with a better hand that has it out-kicked — e.g., **KJ vs. KQ** (you both have a king; their queen kicker crushes your jack). Offsuit broadways are domination magnets: when you flop top pair, the hands that give you action are disproportionately the ones that **beat you** (better kickers, sets, two pair), while the hands you beat tend to fold. This is **reverse implied odds** — you make a hand that looks good, commits chips, and is *second-best* more often than the pot odds suggest.

> **Worked illustration.** You hold **KJo** and flop top pair on a **K-7-3** board. Against a tight opponent who now raises or barrels, what do they have? Rarely a worse king (KT/KQ would often have folded preflop or are themselves ahead), often **AK, KQ** (dominating you), **sets** (77, 33), or **two pair**. Your "top pair" is frequently drawing to **three outs**. You win a small pot when they have a worse hand and fold; you lose a big one when they have a better one and stack you. That asymmetry is the reverse-implied-odds tax on offsuit broadways.

**Offsuit playability.** Being offsuit removes the **flush potential** that makes suited hands flexible. An offsuit broadway essentially *only* makes pairs — no flush draws, fewer straight draws than connectors, no nut-flush upside. So its postflop options are thin: it makes a pair (often a dominated one) or it has little. This is why **suited** broadways (KQs, KJs, AJs) are meaningfully stronger than their offsuit versions and appear earlier in opening and continuing ranges — the flush adds playability and nut potential the offsuit version lacks.

---

## 14.2 A Hierarchy of Broadways

Not all broadways are equal. Roughly, from strongest to weakest:

1. **AK, AQ** — covered in Chapter 11 (premium/strong; AK makes the **best** top pair with the **best** kicker, so it's far less domination-prone). These are the broadways you *want*.
2. **AJ, AT, KQ** — strong-ish, but **kicker-vulnerable** top pairs (AJ's jack kicker loses to AK/AQ on an ace board). Suited versions (AJs, KQs) are clearly better than offsuit.
3. **KJ, QJ, KT, QT, JT** — the **danger zone**: these make top pairs with **mediocre kickers** and are the most domination-prone. Offsuit versions are marginal hands that need position and care; suited versions are saved by flush potential (and JTs/QJs have straight potential too).
4. **Weak offsuit broadways and ace-rag offsuit** (KTo, QTo, A9o, A8o, etc.) — barely playable, late-position-only, and easily folded.

The practical takeaway: **the lower in this hierarchy and the more offsuit, the more cautiously you play, and the more you rely on position to make the hand profitable.** The kicker is everything — top pair with a weak kicker is a trap; top pair with a top kicker (or a hand that can make the nuts) is value.

---

## 14.3 Preflop: Position Is the Filter

Offsuit broadways are **acutely position-dependent** — this is why, in the opening ranges of Chapter 4, **offsuit hands are the last to be added** as you move from UTG to the button, and many offsuit broadways are *only* in the button (and SB/BB-defense) ranges.

- **Early position:** open only the strong broadways (AJo+, KQo from UTG; ATo+, KJo+, QJo from HJ). The weaker offsuit broadways are folds — they're too domination-prone to open into 4–5 players.
- **Late position:** you can open the wider offsuit broadways (KTo, QTo, JTo on the button) as **steals**, because you have position and the blinds fold often — but their value is more in fold equity than in flopping a great hand.
- **Facing opens:** offsuit broadways are **mediocre flat-calls** (domination risk + offsuit = poor postflop equity realization). Prefer to **3-bet them as bluffs or thin value** (when the opponent folds enough or you dominate their range) or **fold**, rather than cold-calling into reverse-implied-odds trouble — especially out of position. The BB can defend some by *calling* (great price closes the action), but elsewhere, lean 3-bet-or-fold.

> **The flatting trap.** Cold-calling offsuit broadways out of position is one of the most common intermediate leaks. You flat KJo, flop top pair, and now you're in a guessing game OOP against a range that has you dominated when it continues. The hand *looks* strong enough to play, but its reverse-implied-odds profile makes it a chronic loser in that spot. Either take initiative (3-bet) or fold; reserve flatting for **position** and for the **BB's discounted defense**.

## 14.4 Postflop: Pot Control Is the Default

When an offsuit broadway flops a pair, your watchword is **pot control** — keep the pot small enough that your kicker-vulnerable top pair doesn't cost you a stack.

### Top pair, weak/medium kicker
- **Bet for thin value and protection on the flop**, but plan to **control the pot** — this is rarely a three-street value hand. A common line is **bet flop, then check or check-call** turn/river rather than barreling into a range that has you dominated when it raises.
- **Don't stack off.** When the money starts going in big (raises, multi-street pressure), your kicker-vulnerable top pair is usually beaten. Fold to credible aggression — paying off AK/AQ/sets/two-pair with KJ is exactly how reverse implied odds drain your stack.
- **In position**, use your position to take free cards and showdown cheaply; **out of position**, check-call as a bluff-catcher and be ready to fold to turn/river barrels.

### Top pair, top kicker (e.g., AQ on Q-high, KQ on Q-high)
Stronger — you can **bet more streets for value** because fewer hands dominate you. Still respect raises (sets, two pair), but these are genuine value hands, not pot-control-only hands. The kicker is what separates "value-bet it" from "pot-control it."

### Missed / second pair / draws
- Offsuit broadways that miss have **overcard equity** (you can pair to a strong top pair) and can **barrel as semi-bluffs** with that backup — similar to AK/AQ but weaker (fewer outs, weaker blockers). On the right board and against fit-or-fold opponents, a c-bet/barrel with two overcards is fine; against stations, give up.
- **Gutshots and the occasional straight draw** (JT, QJ make some straights) add semi-bluff equity — these are the offsuit broadways' best "draw" outcomes, since they have no flush potential.

> **The governing principle.** Offsuit broadways are **bet-fold / pot-control top pairs**, not stack-off hands. Their profit comes from **thin value on the early streets and disciplined folding when the action escalates** — plus occasional overcard semi-bluffs when they miss. Treat a kicker-vulnerable top pair as the medium-strength hand it is (Chapter 3.3's "check the middle"), not as a monster.

---

## 14.5 Multiway: Even More Caution

Offsuit broadways get **substantially worse multiway**:
- Top pair with a weak kicker against **multiple** opponents is far more likely to be dominated or beaten — someone has the better kicker, the set, or the two pair.
- Their lack of flush potential and limited straight potential means they don't make the nutted hands that *do* hold up multiway (unlike suited connectors, Ch. 13).
- **Fold them more preflop multiway**, and **pot-control or fold them faster postflop** when several players are in. A flopped top-pair-weak-kicker multiway is often a check-and-give-up, not a value bet.

---

## 14.6 The Exploitative Layer

- **vs. stations / players who call with worse:** offsuit broadways' **thin value goes up** — value-bet your top pairs a bit more (they pay with worse kickers and second pairs), but still don't stack off your weak kickers into raises.
- **vs. nits / players who only continue with strong hands:** their continuation means you're **dominated** — **pot-control hard and fold to aggression**; don't pay off. Also, their over-folding means you can **3-bet/barrel these as bluffs** preflop and postflop more freely (fold equity replaces thin value).
- **vs. aggressive opponents who barrel/raise a lot:** your kicker-vulnerable top pairs become **bluff-catchers** — call down based on their bluff frequency, but be aware they're representing the exact hands that dominate you; lean toward folding to multi-street pressure unless they're known over-bluffers.
- **vs. fit-or-fold opponents in position:** barrel your **missed** broadways (overcard equity + fold equity) — they fold too much.
- **Always prefer 3-bet-or-fold to flatting offsuit broadways OOP** against competent players, to dodge the reverse-implied-odds trap.

> **The leak this chapter fixes.** The defining offsuit-broadway leak is **overplaying a kicker-vulnerable top pair** — flatting these hands OOP, then stacking off (or hero-calling) when dominated. The fix: **respect domination and reverse implied odds.** Play these hands in position or as the BB's cheap defense, take **thin value early**, **pot-control**, and **fold without ego** when the action says your kicker isn't good enough. Win small, and refuse to lose big.

## 14.7 Chapter Summary

- Offsuit broadways and offsuit holdings make **top pairs with kicker problems** — they suffer **domination** and **reverse implied odds** (win small, lose big), the mirror image of suited connectors.
- Two weaknesses: **domination** (the hands that give action are the ones that beat you) and **offsuit playability** (no flush potential → only makes pairs). **Suited versions are clearly stronger** and appear earlier in ranges.
- **Hierarchy:** AK/AQ (strong, Ch. 11) > AJ/AT/KQ (kicker-vulnerable) > KJ/QJ/KT/QT/JT (danger zone) > weak offsuit broadways/ace-rag (late-position-only). **The kicker decides value-bet vs. pot-control.**
- **Preflop:** acutely **position-dependent** — open strong ones early, wider ones as late steals; **prefer 3-bet-or-fold to flatting OOP** (flatting offsuit broadways OOP is a classic leak); the BB can defend some by calling for the price.
- **Postflop:** **pot control is the default.** Weak-kicker top pairs are **bet-fold** hands (thin value early, fold to escalation), **not stack-off** hands; top-kicker top pairs can value-bet more; missed broadways can **semi-bluff** with overcard/gutshot equity.
- **Multiway:** much worse — fold more preflop, pot-control/fold faster postflop.
- **Exploit:** thin-value more vs. stations (but don't stack off), pot-control and fold vs. nits/aggressors (you're dominated when they continue), barrel misses vs. fit-or-fold, and avoid OOP flats vs. good players.
