# Chapter 18 — Turn Play

The turn is the most underplayed street in poker. Many players have a reasonable flop game and some river instincts, but treat the turn as an afterthought — auto-barreling or auto-checking without re-evaluating. That's a mistake, because the turn is where **pots get big, ranges get narrow, and equities shift hard**. A single turn card can flip who's favored, and the players who *read the card's effect on the ranges* — then barrel the cards that help them and slow down on the cards that help villain — extract enormous value. This chapter is about that skill: turn barreling, equity shifts, and the lines that follow a flop check.

---

## 18.1 What Changes on the Turn

Three things change relative to the flop, and they all push toward **stronger, more polarized ranges**:

1. **Ranges have narrowed.** Everyone who's still in survived a preflop and a flop action. The air has thinned out; the hands that continued are, on average, stronger and more defined. Your reads from the flop action (Ch. 23) sharpen here.
2. **The pot is bigger and the stakes are higher.** A turn bet commits more, sets up a river bet, and threatens stacks. Bluffs cost more; calls cost more. Decisions carry more weight.
3. **One more card has shifted the equities.** Draws either completed, improved, or bricked. The turn card may favor one range over the other dramatically — and *which* range it favors is the central question of turn play.

The practical consequence: **turn betting is more polarized than flop betting.** You bet your strong value and your chosen barrels (often draws/equity hands with the right blockers), and you check more of your medium hands. The "range bet everything small" approach of the flop largely gives way to "bet bigger, more selectively."

---

## 18.2 The Central Skill: Reading Range-Shifting Cards

For every turn card, ask: **whose range does this card help more — mine or villain's?** Then bet the cards that help you and check/slow down on the cards that help villain. This single habit drives correct barreling.

**Cards that favor the aggressor (good barrel cards):**
- **Overcards to the flop that connect with the aggressor's range** — e.g., on a 9-6-3 flop, a turned **K** or **A** helps the PFR (who has lots of broadways/big pairs) far more than the caller. Barrel these.
- **Cards that complete the aggressor's draws or pair their likely holdings.**
- **Blanks on boards the aggressor already had advantage on** — a brick that changes nothing keeps the flop favorite favored; keep barreling a similar range.

**Cards that favor the caller / defender (slow-down cards):**
- **Cards that complete the caller's likely draws** — a third flush card, a card that fills obvious straights on a connected board. These shift equity (and nut advantage) toward the caller; the aggressor should **check more** (and your value hands may need to pot-control).
- **Low/middling connecting cards on boards the caller's range covers better** — e.g., on a 7-6-2 flop, a turned **5** or **8** helps the caller's connected range more than the PFR's.

> **Worked contrast.** You c-bet a flop of **A♦ 8♣ 3♠** (favors you, the PFR) and the BB calls. **Turn K♥:** this *also* favors you — your range gains AK/KK/Ax-two-pair and more, while the BB's calling range (weak aces, 8x, draws) gains little. **Barrel** — often larger — with value and your chosen bluffs (e.g., Q-J with a backdoor that picked up a gutshot, or a club draw). Now rewind: same flop, **turn 7♥** bringing a heart flush draw and a 7-6-5 straighty texture. This card helps the *BB's* range (more draws, more 8x/7x, two-pair makers) more than yours. **Slow down** — check more, bet a tighter value range, and abandon the bluffs that this card just gave the BB equity to call with. Same flop, opposite turn strategy — entirely from *who the card helped*.

## 18.3 Double-Barreling: Which Hands Fire Again

When you c-bet the flop and the turn is a good barrel card, **which hands continue betting?** Your second barrel should be polarized: **value + selected semi-bluffs**, not your whole flop-betting range.

**Barrel for value:** hands strong enough to want a bigger pot and get called by worse — top pair good kicker and better, overpairs, sets, two pair, completed draws.

**Barrel as bluffs/semi-bluffs (choose by equity + blockers):**
- **Draws that retain or gained equity** — a flush draw, an open-ender, or a flop hand that *picked up* a draw on the turn. These are the best barrels: fold equity now, plus a river card to hit.
- **Blocker bluffs** — hands that block villain's continuing range (e.g., you hold a card that blocks their two pair or flush) and unblock their folds.
- **Hands that the turn card lets you credibly represent** — if a K turned and you're representing the K, your bluffs that "make sense" with the K story are more believable.

**Stop barreling (check) with:**
- **Medium hands with showdown value** — second pair, weak top pair. These prefer to **check** and reach showdown / bluff-catch, not bloat the pot.
- **Air that bricked and lost its equity / blocker relevance** — give up. A flop bluff that picks up no equity and no story on a bad turn card should **check-fold**, not fire a second barrel out of momentum.

> **The barrel discipline.** A second barrel is a real commitment of chips into a narrower, stronger range. Fire it with **value and equity/blocker-backed bluffs on cards that favor your range** — and **give up the bluffs that the turn card didn't help.** The leak is **auto-double-barreling** every flop bluff regardless of the turn card or your equity; the fix is selecting barrels by *card + equity + blockers.*

## 18.4 Lines After a Flop Check

Not every turn follows a flop bet. Two important turn lines come after a flop check:

**Delayed c-bet (PFR checked flop, now bets turn).** You checked the flop (medium hand, caller-favoring board, or to protect your checks) and now bet the turn. Use it when:
- The **turn improved your hand or your range** (you can now value-bet or credibly barrel).
- You want to **realize value from a medium hand** you pot-controlled on the flop.
- You're **balancing your flop checks** so they're not all weak.

**Probe bet (caller leads turn after PFR checked back flop).** When the PFR checks back the flop, they've **capped their range** (strong hands usually bet). As the caller, **lead the turn** to attack that capped range (Ch. 10.5) — value bets get called by their medium hands, and bluffs fold out their air. Probing is high-value precisely because the flop check-back is such a reliable weakness tell.

Both lines exploit *shown weakness*: the delayed c-bet leverages your own checked range hitting/improving, and the probe attacks villain's checked-back capped range.

---

## 18.5 Defending Against Turn Barrels

When *you* face a turn barrel, ranges are narrow and the bet is big — discipline matters:

- **Re-anchor on MDF for the new (larger) bet, then adjust.** Turn bets are often bigger, so MDF is lower (you can fold more). Most opponents **under-bluff the turn** (they barrel value and give up air), so **over-fold relative to MDF** unless the player is a known aggressor.
- **Continue with hands that beat the barreling range and block its value.** Your bluff-catchers should ideally block villain's value combos and beat their bluffs. A turned bluff-catcher with no blockers against an under-bluffer is often a fold.
- **Respect the range-shifting card.** If the turn card *helped your range*, you can defend (and raise) more; if it *helped theirs*, fold more — the same card-reading skill, applied defensively. When a scare card that completes draws comes and a passive player suddenly barrels big, believe them.
- **Raise the turn** with strong value (for stacks) and occasionally as a polarized semi-bluff, but turn raises are strong actions — your raising range here should be tight and credible.

---

## 18.6 The Exploitative Layer

- **vs. players who over-fold turns ("one-and-done" flop callers):** **double-barrel relentlessly** — the turn is where their marginal flop-calls surrender. This is one of the most profitable exploits in 6-max.
- **vs. calling stations:** **stop barreling bluffs** on the turn (they call) and **value-bet bigger** — extract from the hands they won't fold. Your draws become implied-odds calls, not barrels.
- **vs. under-bluffers (most players) when you're facing a barrel:** **over-fold** your bluff-catchers — their turn aggression is value-heavy.
- **vs. aggressive barrelers/maniacs:** **call down wider** (they're bluffing enough) and consider **floating the turn** to take it on the river, or check-raising as a bluff-catcher-plus.
- **vs. players who check back flop too much (capping themselves):** **probe the turn** aggressively — they fold their capped, weak ranges.

> **The leak this chapter fixes.** The turn is where most players run on autopilot: **auto-barreling every flop bluff** regardless of the card (spewing into improved caller ranges) and **auto-calling turn bets** with hands that can't beat a value-heavy barreling range (paying off under-bluffers). The fix: **read the range-shifting card, barrel selectively (value + equity/blocker bluffs on cards that favor you), and defend tightly (over-fold vs. under-bluffers, respect scare cards).**

---

## 18.7 Chapter Summary

- The turn brings **narrower ranges, bigger pots, and shifted equities** → betting is **more polarized** than the flop (bet value + selected barrels, check more medium hands).
- **Central skill:** read **range-shifting cards** — *whose range does this card help?* **Barrel the cards that favor you** (overcards/blanks on boards you already lead), **slow down on cards that favor villain** (flush/straight completers, low connectors on caller-favoring boards).
- **Double-barrel** a **polarized** range: value + **semi-bluffs with retained/new equity and good blockers** on favorable cards; **give up** medium showdown hands (check) and bricked air (check-fold). Don't auto-barrel.
- **After a flop check:** **delayed c-bet** (your checked range improved/wants value) and **probe bet** (attack the PFR's capped check-back range) — both exploit shown weakness.
- **Defending:** re-anchor on MDF for the bigger bet, **over-fold vs. under-bluffers**, continue with blockers-to-value bluff-catchers, **respect scare cards**, and keep turn raises tight and credible.
- **Exploit:** double-barrel over-folders, value-bet (don't bluff) stations, over-fold vs. under-bluffers, call wide vs. maniacs, and probe self-capping check-backers.
