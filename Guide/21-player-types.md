# Chapter 21 — Player Types & How to Exploit Them

Part VI is your second priority: **reading villains**. Everything in Parts I–V gave you a balanced baseline; this Part teaches you to identify how each specific opponent *deviates* from that baseline so you can punish them. We start with the most useful mental model in all of poker: classifying players into a small number of **archetypes**. You'll rarely know an opponent's exact strategy, but within a few orbits you can place them into a type — and each type has a known, repeatable set of mistakes and a corresponding game plan. This chapter defines the archetypes, shows how to spot them fast, and gives the exploit blueprint for each.

---

## 21.1 The Two Axes: Loose/Tight and Passive/Aggressive

Every player can be located on two axes, and the combination defines their type:

- **Loose ↔ Tight** = *how many hands they play* (their range width). Loose players enter lots of pots with weak hands; tight players play few, strong hands.
- **Passive ↔ Aggressive** = *how they play those hands* (bet/raise vs. check/call). Passive players check and call; aggressive players bet and raise.

Crossing the two axes gives four quadrants, plus the all-important distinction of whether a player is **thinking/winning (a "reg")** or **recreational (a "fish")**. The four classic labels:

| | **Passive** | **Aggressive** |
|---|-------------|----------------|
| **Tight** | **Nit** (Tight-Passive) | **TAG** (Tight-Aggressive) |
| **Loose** | **Calling Station** (Loose-Passive) | **LAG / Maniac** (Loose-Aggressive) |

Your first read on any new opponent is to place them in a quadrant. The **HUD stats** of Chapter 22 (VPIP, PFR, AF) quantify exactly these two axes; this chapter is the qualitative version you can do by observation even without a HUD (essential for live and short samples).

## 21.2 The Archetypes

### The Nit (Tight-Passive)
Plays very few hands, and plays them passively — checks and calls, rarely bluffs, only bets/raises with strong hands. The "weak-tight" player.
- **Tells:** low pot involvement, folds to most aggression, suddenly bets/raises only when they have it. When a nit shows aggression, **believe them**.
- **Exploit:** **Steal relentlessly** — open and 3-bet wide, c-bet their blinds, attack their over-folding. **Fold to their aggression** (their bets/raises are value) — don't pay off, don't bluff-catch. **Stop bluffing** them on later streets *only when they show strength*; but their flop/preflop over-folding means **bet-bluff them a lot when they're passive.** They surrender the small pots; just take them.

### The TAG (Tight-Aggressive)
Plays a tight, strong range *aggressively* — the classic solid winning style and probably what you're aiming to be. Selective but applies pressure.
- **Tells:** reasonable, position-aware ranges; c-bets and barrels with logic; doesn't spew.
- **Exploit:** the hardest type to exploit (they're playing well). **Respect their ranges**, avoid marginal spots, and look for the *specific* leaks even good TAGs have (over-c-betting, over-folding the BB, predictable sizing). Against a strong TAG, **lean toward your balanced baseline** — don't try to out-fancy a competent player; pick up small edges and avoid big mistakes. Get **position** on them and let them make the errors.

### The Calling Station (Loose-Passive)
Plays lots of hands and **calls too much** — won't fold, won't raise, "calls to see what happens." The recreational player who pays you off.
- **Tells:** calls down with weak hands, rarely raises, hard to bluff off anything, sees lots of showdowns.
- **Exploit:** the **most profitable type.** **Value-bet relentlessly and thinly** — bet bigger, more streets, with thinner hands; they pay. **Never bluff them** — they don't fold. **Don't try to be fancy**; just make hands and bet them. Set-mine and play implied-odds hands against them (they stack off). Against stations, poker becomes "make a hand, bet it big, repeat."

### The LAG (Loose-Aggressive) and the Maniac
Plays many hands *aggressively* — lots of opens, 3-bets, barrels, bluffs. A skilled **LAG** is a tough, high-variance winner; an out-of-control **Maniac** is a spew machine.
- **Tells:** high pot involvement *with* high aggression; frequent 3-bets and barrels; bluffs a lot.
- **Exploit:** **Tighten up and let them hang themselves.** Stop bluffing (they don't fold and they re-raise); instead **value-bet and bluff-catch wider** (their aggression is bluff-heavy, so call down lighter and trap with strong hands — let them bet *into* you). **Re-raise for value** more (their wide ranges are often weak). Don't get into bluffing wars with a maniac — **value-own them** by making hands and letting them barrel into you. Against a *skilled* LAG, you need tighter discipline and position; against a *maniac*, just wait for hands and call/raise.

> **The reg vs. fish overlay.** Within these quadrants, distinguish **thinking regulars** (who adjust, balance, and counter-exploit) from **recreational players** (who make the same mistakes repeatedly and don't adapt). Against **regs**, deviate *cautiously* (they'll punish over-deviation) and lean on your baseline. Against **fish**, deviate *hard* — they won't adjust, so milk the exploit indefinitely. **Most of your profit comes from a small number of fish**, so identifying and targeting them (and getting position on them) is the highest-value thing you do at the table.

## 21.3 How to Classify Fast (Without a Full Sample)

You won't have 10,000 hands on everyone. Classify quickly from limited information:

- **First few hands:** how many pots do they enter (loose/tight) and are they betting/raising or checking/calling (aggressive/passive)? Two or three showdowns tell you a lot about range width and aggression.
- **Showdowns are gold:** every time a player shows their hand, update your read — *that's* the hand they took *that* line with. A station showing down ace-high after calling three streets, a nit showing the nuts after raising — these calibrate your model fast.
- **Default assumption:** absent any read, assume a new opponent is a **slightly-loose, somewhat-passive recreational player** (the modal 6-max opponent, especially at low/mid stakes) until they prove otherwise. This default already points you toward value-betting and not over-bluffing.
- **Bet sizing and timing** (Ch. 24) give early reads even before showdowns — transparent sizing and timing tells often reveal type within a few hands.

## 21.4 Adjusting Your Own Image and Table Selection

Reading villains also means understanding how they read **you**, and choosing **who you play**:

- **Your image:** if you've been card-dead and folding, observant opponents see you as a nit — your eventual bets get more respect (bluff more). If you've been splashing around, your value bets get paid (bluff less, value-bet more). Adjust to how the table perceives you, *but only against opponents paying attention* — against fish, your image is irrelevant because they're not tracking it.
- **Table & seat selection (cash):** the single highest-value application of player typing. **Sit to the left of the fish and the aggressive players** (act after them), keep the **calling stations on your right** so you isolate them in position, and **leave tables full of tough TAGs/regs** with no fish — there's no money there. Choosing *who* you play often matters more than how well you play.

## 21.5 The Exploit Cheat Sheet

| Type | Their core mistake | Your counter |
|------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Nit** | Folds too much, only bets with the nuts | Steal/bluff their passivity relentlessly; **fold to their aggression** |
| **TAG** | Few exploitable errors (small leaks only) | Respect, get position, play your baseline, punish their *specific* leaks |
| **Station** | Calls too much, never folds, never raises | **Value-bet thin and big; never bluff**; set-mine/implied-odds hands |
| **LAG** | Over-aggressive, bluffs too much, wide ranges | Tighten, **bluff-catch and value-bet wider**, let them barrel into you; don't bluff-fight |
| **Maniac** | Spews, barrels everything | Wait for hands, **call/raise for value**, trap; stop bluffing entirely |

> **The master key.** Two questions classify any opponent and dictate your strategy: **"How often do they fold?"** and **"How aggressive are they?"** Over-folders → bluff them and fold to their rare aggression. Under-folders (stations) → value-bet them and never bluff. Over-aggressors → bluff-catch and value-own them. Under-aggressors (passive) → steal from them and respect their occasional bets. Every exploit in this guide reduces to reading these two tendencies and bending your baseline toward the punishment.

## 21.6 Chapter Summary

- Place every opponent on two axes: **loose/tight** (range width) and **passive/aggressive** (bet-raise vs. check-call). The quadrants give the classic types: **Nit** (tight-passive), **TAG** (tight-aggressive), **Calling Station** (loose-passive), **LAG/Maniac** (loose-aggressive).
- **Nit:** steal/bluff relentlessly, **fold to their aggression.** **TAG:** respect, get position, play baseline, punish specific leaks. **Station (most profitable):** **value-bet thin/big, never bluff.** **LAG/Maniac:** tighten, **bluff-catch and value-bet wider, let them barrel into you, don't bluff-fight.**
- Overlay **reg vs. fish:** deviate cautiously vs. adapting regs, **hard and indefinitely vs. non-adapting fish** — most profit comes from a few fish, so target and position yourself against them.
- **Classify fast** from pot frequency, aggression, and especially **showdowns**; default to "slightly loose, somewhat passive recreational" absent a read.
- **Manage your image** (only vs. attentive opponents) and **select tables/seats** — sit left of fish and aggressors, keep stations on your right, leave tough fishless tables.
- **Master key:** read **"how often do they fold?"** and **"how aggressive are they?"** — every exploit follows from those two answers.
