# Chapter 24 — Tells: Sizing, Timing & Physical

A **tell** is any observable behavior that leaks information about a player's hand beyond their bet-sizing-and-action story. Tells layer *on top of* the action-reading from Chapter 23 — they don't replace it, they refine it, often tipping close decisions. This chapter covers the three categories, in order of reliability and applicability: **sizing tells** (available online and live, highly reliable against weak players), **timing tells** (available everywhere, moderately reliable), and **physical tells** (live only, least reliable but real). It closes with the crucial caveat that tells are *probabilistic evidence*, not certainties — and that against strong players, many "tells" are deliberate reverse-tells.

---

## 24.1 Sizing Tells (The Most Useful)

How much an opponent bets often leaks their hand strength — especially among recreational players who size by "how I feel about my hand" rather than by strategy. Sizing tells are available **online and live** and are the most actionable category.

**Common sizing tells in weaker players:**
- **Big bet = strong, small bet = weak** (the most common, most reliable pattern). Many recreational players bet big when they have it and small when they're weak or bluffing/thin. Against such a player: **fold marginal hands to big bets, and call/raise their small bets** more freely.
- **The "blocker bet" / small stab** — a small bet (often 20–25% pot) into you, frequently meaning a **medium hand that wants a cheap showdown** or a weak hand trying to avoid a bigger bet. Against weak players this often signals "marginal, please don't raise me" → **raise it as a bluff** (they fold the marginal hand) or call lightly. (Note: strong players *also* use small blocker bets deliberately — read by type.)
- **Overbet from a recreational player** → almost always **the nuts or near-nuts** (they don't overbet as bluffs) → **fold everything but a hand that beats their value.**
- **Sudden sizing change** — a player who's been betting 1/2 pot suddenly bets pot, or shrinks to a min-bet, is leaking. A jump *up* in size often means a stronger hand (or a draw wanting fold equity); a jump *down* often means weakness or a thin value/showdown-seeking hand. Note *deviations from their own baseline*.
- **Bet sizing tied to hand category** — some players use a recognizable big size only for their draws/bluffs (to maximize fold equity) and a different size for value, or vice versa. Once you spot the pattern, you've solved them.

> **Why sizing tells are gold online.** You can't see a player's face online, but you can see **every bet size** — and weak players' sizing is shockingly transparent. Tracking "what does this player's big bet mean vs. their small bet?" — confirmed by showdowns — is the single most powerful online read after the HUD stats. Most of the player pool sizes by hand strength, not by strategy, so their sizes *are* their hand.

## 24.2 Timing Tells

How *long* a player takes to act leaks information — available **online and live**. Timing tells are real but noisier than sizing tells (affected by multi-tabling, distraction, internet lag), so weight them moderately.

**Common timing tells:**
- **Instant bet / snap bet** → often a **planned action** — either a pre-decided value bet, a draw they always bet, or (on later streets) a hand they were always betting. An instant *big* bet can signal a strong, pre-planned line. An instant *call* often means a **medium hand / draw** (they didn't need to think — it's an easy continue, not a strong hand that might raise).
- **Instant check** → usually **weakness / giving up** (they checked the moment it was their turn — no decision to make because they have nothing to bet).
- **Long pause, then bet** → context-dependent: genuinely tough decision, or (a known reverse-tell) **Hollywooding** — acting weak/uncertain with a strong hand to induce a call. Among honest recreational players, a long think before a big bet is often a *genuinely* close value decision or a real hand; among trickier players, beware the act.
- **Long pause, then check** → often a **real decision** between betting and checking a medium hand — usually genuine weakness-to-medium.
- **Tank then call** → frequently a **marginal/bluff-catch** call (they agonized, decided to call) — a capped, medium range.

> **The "Hollywood" reverse-tell.** A classic deliberate timing tell: **acting opposite to hand strength.** A player who **sighs, hesitates, and reluctantly bets** is often *strong* (faking weakness to get called); a player who **acts strong, confident, and snappy** can be *weak* (faking strength to get a fold). This "strong means weak, weak means strong" pattern is real among players trying to deceive — but it's exactly why you **weight timing tells by player type**: honest recreational players' timing is literal, while trickier players' timing may be theater. Don't over-trust any single timing tell.

## 24.3 Physical Tells (Live Only)

In live poker, body language adds a layer — but it's the **least reliable** category, heavily romanticized by TV, and easily faked by anyone aware of it. Use physical tells as **minor tiebreakers**, never as primary evidence, and always behind action-reading and sizing/timing.

**Some reasonably documented patterns (recreational/unaware players):**
- **Genuine relaxation = strength; tension = weakness/bluff** — a common framing. A player who's loose, talkative, and comfortable after betting is often **strong** (relaxed because they have it); a player who's rigid, holding their breath, avoiding eye contact, or with a throbbing/visible pulse after betting is often **bluffing** (stress response). Caution: some interpretations of "weak means strong" run the opposite way for *acting* players — read the individual.
- **Trembling hands when betting** → classically associated with **a big made hand** (adrenaline/excitement), more often than a bluff (counterintuitively). But this is individual.
- **Chip-handling and bet-placement** — forceful, aggressive chip-splashing can be a weak player *acting* strong; gentle, careful placement can accompany genuine strength. Again, often reversed for deliberate actors.
- **Eye contact / staring** — some players stare down opponents when bluffing (challenge), look away when strong (don't want to scare you off) — but this is highly individual and easily reversed.
- **Pre-flop glance at chips after seeing cards** → a player who **looks at their stack right after looking at their cards** is often planning to play (interested in a strong hand) — a "weak" interest tell.

> **The honest truth about physical tells.** They are **real but weak, individual, and reversible.** TV overstates them; serious players neutralize them (sunglasses, hoodies, stillness, deliberate reverse-acts). Treat any physical read as a **small nudge** to a decision you'd otherwise make on action-reading — never as a reason to override the math or the range. The most reliable "physical" tells are actually **behavioral patterns over time** (this player always glances at the flop when they've connected; this player only reaches for chips early when bluffing), confirmed by showdowns — the live equivalent of building a HUD by observation.

## 24.4 Reliability, Baselines, and Reverse-Tells

Three principles govern *using* tells without getting trapped:

1. **Tells are probabilistic, not deterministic.** A tell shifts the odds; it doesn't reveal the hand. Use tells to **break close decisions** and to **confirm or contradict your action-read** — not to make wild plays. If the range math says fold and a weak tell says "maybe bluff," it's a close call, not an auto-call.
2. **Read deviations from the player's own baseline.** A tell is meaningful only relative to how that player **normally** acts. A naturally slow player betting instantly is informative; a naturally fast player betting instantly is not. Establish each opponent's baseline (speed, sizing, demeanor) before reading deviations from it.
3. **Filter by player type (always).** Against **recreational/unaware players**, tells are **honest and literal** — exploit them directly (their big bet is strong, their snap-check is weak, their tension is a bluff). Against **aware/strong players**, assume **reverse-tells and deliberate balance** — their "tells" may be theater, so discount them and rely on action-reading and frequencies. *Who* is giving the tell determines whether to trust it.

> **The integration rule.** Tells sit *on top of* the hand-reading from Chapter 23, not instead of it. The process is: build villain's range from the action (Ch. 23), count combos vs. pot odds (Ch. 2, 23), then **use sizing/timing/physical tells to nudge close spots** — weighted by reliability (sizing > timing > physical) and by player type (literal for fish, suspect for regs). A tell is the tiebreaker, not the verdict.

## 24.5 The Exploitative Layer

- **vs. recreational players with transparent sizing:** this is the biggest practical edge — **fold to their big bets, attack their small bets, fold to their overbets, raise their blocker bets.** Their sizes are their hands; play accordingly and confirm via showdowns.
- **vs. players with honest timing:** trust **snap-check = weak** (attack it) and **snap big bet = planned strength** (respect it); read **tank-calls as capped** (they can be barreled/overbet next street).
- **vs. unaware live players:** use **relaxation = strong, tension = bluff** as a minor tiebreaker, and bank **behavioral patterns** over time confirmed by showdowns.
- **vs. aware/strong players:** **largely ignore physical and theatrical timing tells** (assume reverse-tells); neutralize your *own* tells by keeping consistent sizing, timing, and demeanor; rely on action-reading and balance.
- **Protect your own information:** use **consistent bet sizes and timing** so you don't leak — especially against observant opponents. The same way you read their sizing/timing, good players read yours.

> **The leak this chapter fixes.** Two opposite tell leaks: **(1) over-trusting tells** — making big, math-defying plays off a single twitchy "read," especially against aware players running reverse-tells — and **(2) ignoring transparent sizing/timing tells** that recreational players hand you for free. The fix: **rank tells by reliability (sizing > timing > physical), read them as deviations from each player's baseline, trust them literally vs. fish and suspiciously vs. regs, and use them only to break close spots — never to override the range and the math.**

## 24.6 Chapter Summary

- Tells **layer on top of** action-reading (Ch. 23) — they refine close decisions, they don't replace range analysis. Reliability ranking: **sizing > timing > physical.**
- **Sizing tells** (online + live, most useful): weak players bet **big = strong, small = weak**; **overbets = the nuts**; **small "blocker" stabs = medium/weak** (raise them); note **deviations from their baseline size**. Most of the pool sizes by hand strength — their size is their hand.
- **Timing tells** (online + live, moderate): **snap bet = planned**, **snap check = weak**, **snap call = medium/draw**, **tank-then-call = capped/marginal**; beware the **"Hollywood" reverse-tell** (act weak = strong, act strong = weak) from deceptive players.
- **Physical tells** (live only, least reliable): **relaxation = strength, tension = bluff** as a rough frame, but **individual and reversible**; the best live reads are **behavioral patterns over time confirmed by showdowns**. Don't override math with body language.
- **Using tells safely:** they're **probabilistic**, read as **deviations from each player's baseline**, and **filtered by type** — **literal vs. recreational players, suspect (reverse-tells) vs. strong ones.** A tell is the **tiebreaker, not the verdict.**
- **Exploit** transparent recreational sizing/timing directly; ignore theatrical tells from aware players; and **protect your own** consistent sizing/timing/demeanor.
