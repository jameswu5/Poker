# Appendix — Glossary

Definitions of every key term used in the guide. Chapter references point to where the concept is developed.

---

## Positions & Table

- **6-max** — six-handed table; the format this guide covers. (Ch. 1)
- **UTG (Under the Gun)** — the first seat to act preflop; the only early position in 6-max. (Ch. 1)
- **HJ (Hijack)** — the middle position, two seats right of the button. (Ch. 1)
- **CO (Cutoff)** — late position, immediately right of the button. (Ch. 1)
- **BTN (Button)** — the best seat; acts last postflop. (Ch. 1)
- **SB / BB (Small/Big Blind)** — the two forced-bet seats, out of position postflop; the BB closes preflop action. (Ch. 1, 7)
- **IP / OOP (In/Out of Position)** — acting last / not last on postflop streets. (Ch. 1)
- **Effective stack** — the smaller of the two stacks in a confrontation; the most either can win or lose. (Ch. 1)
- **Blind vs. Blind (BvB)** — a pot contested only between SB and BB. (Ch. 7)

## Preflop Actions

- **RFI (Raise First In)** — open-raising when everyone before you has folded. (Ch. 4)
- **Open / 2-bet** — the first raise. **3-bet** — a re-raise. **4-bet** — a re-re-raise. **5-bet** — usually all-in at 100bb. (Ch. 4–6)
- **Limp** — calling the big blind preflop instead of raising (generally a mistake in 6-max). (Ch. 4)
- **Cold-call** — flatting a raise with players still to act behind you. (Ch. 7)
- **Squeeze** — a 3-bet after an open *and* one or more callers. (Ch. 7)
- **Isolate** — raising to play heads-up against a weak player (often a limper). (Ch. 27)
- **Steal** — a (usually late-position) open aimed at winning the blinds uncontested. (Ch. 4)
- **Re-steal / re-shove** — 3-betting or jamming over a steal, often as a bluff/semi-bluff. (Ch. 7, 29)

## Postflop Actions

- **C-bet (continuation bet)** — a flop bet by the preflop raiser. (Ch. 9)
- **Delayed c-bet** — checking the flop, then betting the turn. (Ch. 9, 18)
- **Donk-bet** — the caller leading into the preflop raiser on the flop. (Ch. 10)
- **Probe bet** — the caller leading the turn after the PFR checked back the flop. (Ch. 10, 18)
- **Float** — calling a bet with a weak hand, planning to take the pot later (usually IP). (Ch. 10)
- **Check-raise** — checking, then raising when villain bets. (Ch. 10)
- **Barrel** — a continued bet on a later street (double-barrel = turn; triple-barrel = river). (Ch. 18)
- **Slowplay** — underplaying a strong hand (checking/calling) to disguise it. (Ch. 11)
- **Pot control** — keeping the pot small with a medium-strength hand. (Ch. 11, 14)

## Hand Types

- **Premium pairs** — AA, KK, QQ. (Ch. 11)
- **Overpair** — a pocket pair higher than every board card. **Underpair** — lower than a board card. (Ch. 11, 12)
- **Set** — three of a kind using a pocket pair (two of your cards). **Trips** — three of a kind using one hole card + a paired board. (Ch. 12)
- **Set-mining** — calling preflop with a small pair hoping to flop a set. (Ch. 12)
- **Suited connector / gapper** — consecutive (or near-consecutive) same-suit cards (e.g., 87s / 86s). (Ch. 13)
- **Suited ace** — an ace with a same-suit kicker; A5s–A2s ("wheel aces") are key bluff-blockers. (Ch. 13)
- **Broadway** — a hand made of T–A cards; offsuit broadways are domination-prone. (Ch. 14)
- **Top pair top kicker (TPTK)** — top pair with the best possible kicker. (Ch. 11, 14)
- **Draw** — a hand that can improve to a strong made hand (flush/straight draws). (Ch. 15)
- **OESD** — open-ended straight draw (8 outs). **Gutshot** — inside straight draw (4 outs). **Combo draw** — flush + straight draw together. (Ch. 15)
- **Bluff-catcher** — a hand that beats villain's bluffs but loses to their value. (Ch. 10, 16, 19)

## Ranges & Theory

- **Range** — the complete set of hands a player can hold in a situation. (Ch. 3)
- **Linear range** — the best N% of hands, top-down (value, no bluffs). (Ch. 3, 5)
- **Polarized range** — value (nuts/near-nuts) + bluffs, excluding medium hands. (Ch. 3, 5)
- **Merged range** — value-dense, includes medium hands but few pure bluffs. (Ch. 3)
- **Range advantage** — whose entire range has more equity on a board (→ c-bet frequency). (Ch. 3, 8)
- **Nut advantage** — who holds more of the strongest hands (→ bet sizing). (Ch. 3, 8)
- **Capped / uncapped** — a range that lacks (or still contains) the nutted hands. (Ch. 3, 23)
- **GTO (Game Theory Optimal)** — the unexploitable equilibrium strategy. (Ch. 3, 25)
- **Exploitative play** — deviating from GTO to punish a specific opponent's mistakes. (Ch. 3, 26)
- **Indifference** — making the opponent's options equal in EV, the mechanism behind GTO frequencies. (Ch. 25)
- **Mixed strategy** — taking an action a fraction of the time (arises at indifference). (Ch. 25)
- **Balance** — having the right value:bluff ratio so you can't be exploited. (Ch. 2, 25)

## Math

- **Equity** — your share of the pot at showdown (% you win + half the ties). (Ch. 2)
- **Equity realization** — how much of your raw equity you actually capture (>100% IP, <100% OOP). (Ch. 1, 2)
- **Pot odds** — the price to continue: amount to call ÷ (call + total pot). (Ch. 2)
- **Implied odds** — extra money won on later streets when you hit. **Reverse implied odds** — extra money lost when you make a second-best hand. (Ch. 2, 14)
- **EV (Expected Value)** — the average result of a decision over the long run. (Ch. 2)
- **Outs** — cards that improve you to (likely) the best hand. (Ch. 2, 15)
- **Rule of 2 and 4** — equity ≈ outs × 4 (flop, two cards) or × 2 (turn, one card). (Ch. 2)
- **MDF (Minimum Defense Frequency)** — the minimum % of your range you must continue to stop auto-profit bluffs = pot ÷ (pot + bet). (Ch. 2, 10)
- **Alpha** — how often a bluff must work to break even = bet ÷ (bet + pot) = 1 − MDF. (Ch. 2)
- **Combinatorics (combos)** — counting hand combinations (16 per unpaired hand, 6 per pair). (Ch. 2, 23)
- **Blocker** — a card you hold that reduces the combos of hands villain can have. **Unblocker** — not removing combos of villain's folding range. (Ch. 2, 16, 19)
- **Overbet** — a bet larger than the pot. **Geometric sizing** — equal pot-fractions each street to get stacks in by the river. (Ch. 20)

## Player Types & Reads

- **Nit** — tight-passive (folds too much, only bets the nuts). (Ch. 21)
- **TAG** — tight-aggressive (the solid winning style). (Ch. 21)
- **Calling station** — loose-passive (calls too much, never folds). (Ch. 21)
- **LAG / Maniac** — loose-aggressive / out-of-control aggressive. (Ch. 21)
- **Reg / Fish** — a thinking regular / a recreational player. (Ch. 21)
- **HUD** — Heads-Up Display showing opponents' stats. (Ch. 22)
- **VPIP** — % of hands voluntarily played (looseness). **PFR** — % raised preflop (aggression). (Ch. 22)
- **3-bet % / Fold to 3-bet / C-bet % / Fold to c-bet** — preflop and flop tendency stats. (Ch. 22)
- **AF (Aggression Factor)** — (bets + raises) ÷ calls (postflop aggression). (Ch. 22)
- **WTSD (Went To Showdown %) / W£SD (Won £ at Showdown %)** — showdown frequency and quality. (Ch. 22)
- **Tell** — observable behavior leaking hand strength (sizing, timing, physical). (Ch. 24)

## Tournament Terms

- **ICM (Independent Chip Model)** — converts chip stacks to real-money equity; chips lost are worth more than chips won. (Ch. 29)
- **Push/fold** — short-stack strategy of jamming or folding (≤ ~15bb). (Ch. 29)
- **Bubble** — the spot just before the money/pay jumps, where ICM pressure peaks. (Ch. 29)
- **Antes** — forced bets from all players in later levels; add dead money and widen steals. (Ch. 29)
- **Risk of ruin** — the probability of going broke given your edge and bankroll. (Ch. 28)

## Mental Game

- **Variance** — the natural short-term swings of a high-luck game. (Ch. 28)
- **Bankroll management (BRM)** — keeping enough buy-ins to survive variance (30–50 for cash). (Ch. 28)
- **Tilt** — letting emotion degrade your decisions. (Ch. 28)
- **Stop-loss / stop-time** — pre-set limits for quitting a session. (Ch. 28)
